Overview

Treatment with medicines, chosen for you

Medical oncology is the treatment of cancer with medicines. Depending on the type and stage of cancer, these may be used to shrink a tumour before surgery, to lower the chance of the cancer returning after surgery, or to control the disease and relieve symptoms.

Our medical oncologists choose the treatment based on your diagnosis, your general health and your own wishes, and they explain the expected benefits and side effects before anything begins.

Available treatments

Chemotherapy

Medicines that destroy cancer cells, given in planned cycles in our day-care unit.

Targeted therapy

Medicines that act on specific features of cancer cells, where tests show they will help.

Immunotherapy

Treatment that helps the body's own immune system fight the cancer.

Hormone therapy

Used for cancers such as breast and prostate cancer that respond to hormones.

Supportive care

Treatment of pain, nausea, infection and other symptoms during therapy.

Follow-up care

Regular reviews after treatment to monitor recovery and detect problems early.

The list of treatments will be confirmed with the hospital's medical oncology team.

What to expect

ConsultationThe oncologist reviews your reports and explains the diagnosis and the treatment options.
Treatment planYou receive a written plan with the medicines, the number of cycles and the likely costs.
Day-care treatmentMost chemotherapy is given in our day-care unit, and you go home the same day.
Between cyclesOur team stays in touch about side effects, and the dietitian adjusts your diet as needed.
ReviewScans and tests show how well the treatment is working, and the plan is adjusted if required.

Support during chemotherapy

  • Specialised nutrition. A dedicated oncology dietitian plans your diet during chemotherapy and recovery.
  • Psychological support. Professional counselling for patients and their families.
  • Right guidance. Honest advice that helps you avoid unnecessary investigations and expenses.
  • Family education. We teach caregivers how to help at home and when to call us.

Frequently asked questions

Will I need to be admitted for chemotherapy?

Most chemotherapy is given as a day-care treatment. Some schedules need a short admission, and your doctor will tell you in advance.

What side effects should I expect?

Side effects depend on the medicines used. Common ones include tiredness, nausea and a higher risk of infection. Most can be prevented or treated, and we explain what to watch for before each cycle.

What should I eat during treatment?

Good nutrition helps you tolerate treatment and recover. Our oncology dietitian gives each patient a personal plan.

Can I continue working during treatment?

Many patients do, with some adjustments. It depends on your treatment and how you feel, and your doctor can advise you.
