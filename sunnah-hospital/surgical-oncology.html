Overview

Surgery planned around the whole patient

Our skilled surgical team provides advanced and safe surgical solutions for a wide range of cancers.

For many cancers, surgery offers the best chance of cure. A surgical oncologist is a surgeon specially trained to operate on cancer: to remove the tumour with a safe margin, to examine the nearby lymph nodes, and to preserve as much normal function as possible.

At Sunnah Hospital, surgery is never planned alone. Our surgical and medical oncology teams decide together whether surgery should come first, or after chemotherapy, and what further treatment may be needed.

Surgical services

Head & Neck Cancer Surgery

Surgery for cancers of the mouth, throat and thyroid.

Breast Cancer Surgery

Breast-conserving surgery where suitable, and mastectomy when needed.

Gastrointestinal Cancer Surgery

Surgery for cancers of the stomach, intestine and related organs.

Gynecological Cancer Surgery

Surgery for cancers of the cervix, ovary and uterus.

Operations are performed in our Modular Operation Theatre, with a Minor OT for procedures and ICU and HDU support after surgery.

What to expect

ConsultationThe surgeon reviews your reports, examines you and explains the options in plain language.
Planning & fitness testsYour treatment plan is discussed by the team, and tests are done to make sure you are fit for surgery.
SurgeryYour operation is carried out by the surgical oncology team, with anaesthesia and nursing staff experienced in cancer care.
Recovery in hospitalWe help you move, eat and manage pain as early as it is safe, with our dietitian guiding your nutrition.
Follow-upYour final report is explained to you, along with any further treatment and a schedule of check-ups.

Support around your surgery

  • Right guidance. Clear advice on which tests and treatments you need, and which you do not.
  • Counselling. Emotional support for you and your family before and after the operation.
  • Nutrition after surgery. A personalised diet plan from our oncology dietitian.
  • Recovery guidance. Advice on wound care, activity and returning to daily life.

Frequently asked questions

Does every cancer patient need surgery?

No. It depends on the type and stage of cancer. Some patients are treated with surgery alone, some with medicines, and many with a combination. Your doctors will explain what is right for you.

How long will I stay in hospital?

This depends on the operation and on your general health. Your surgeon will give you an estimate before admission.

Will I need chemotherapy after surgery?

Sometimes. The decision is made after the removed tissue has been examined in the laboratory, and your doctors will discuss it with you.

What should I bring to the first consultation?

All your reports, scans and biopsy slides if you have them, a list of your medicines, and a family member if possible.
